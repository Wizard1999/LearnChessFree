# chess.com research: closing synthesis for Scholarmate (9 Nov 2026)

Scope [V]: the whole research series, i.e. the Learn path (59 tiles), the new-player journey, the controls audit, and 282 of the 395 library courses marked sampled, played or spot-checked in the inventory (segments 5 to 14; the other 113 are near-duplicate families, each checked through one member, first challenge of lesson 1 for most; engine-checked with the local Stockfish 19 lite at depth 14 to 20). Detail and evidence live in the `research-chesscom-*` files named below; this file only ranks the conclusions. Labels: [V] seen on chess.com and checked here, [S] secondary, [I] inference for Scholarmate. Wording is original; no chess.com text beyond short feedback phrases is reproduced. Not covered: lessons beyond the first challenge for most courses, paywalled lessons, audio and video content, and long-run retention (a trial cannot show it).

## Adopt (ranked by value for a new learner and for Andrew)

1. **Three-state feedback that names the cost: right, "also works", wrong, with a line the learner can replay.** The best texts state the refutation or the cost: "After Qa6 and Nxd4 Black wins back the pawn" (Materialistic, seg. 11-12), "ties down both your queen and knight" (Restrict and Win, seg. 12), "that's fine, but it's not the Evans Gambit" (Evans Gambit, seg. 13), "this is OK, but Sultan Khan focused on the kingside" (Indian School, seg. 13) [V]. Store per item `accepted`, `alt` (with its one-line cost) and `why_wrong` per distractor.
2. **Hints that give a goal or class, not a square, in a short ladder.** "Look for a way to expose the black king" (King's Indian Attack), "look for a desperado sacrifice" (Jan's Four Knights), "a better capture" (Greco, Tarrasch) [V, seg. 13]; ladder: concept word, then highlighted piece, then arrow (Strategies for Rapid Chess, seg. 7-8) [V]. Count hint presses as evidence for fading support [I].
3. **Multi-step challenges that keep the learner in the line.** A right move produces a follow-up question inside the same position ("How should you recapture?", "How did he continue to gain space?") [V]. It is the cheapest "visible next want" there is, about 40 characters, and the model for Scholarmate's guided games. The best full lesson seen (Tatev's Favorite Studies, seg. 14) runs one study three ways: a build-up with scripted replies, a branch (C4), then a stripped-down transfer item whose prompt names the shared motif (C5) [V].
4. **Goal-in-the-prompt items that accept every move meeting the goal.** Seen in Old School Cool, Master Tactics, Grind Like Magnus (seg. 8-12) [V]. Store a goal predicate and let `validate.py` enumerate the matches.
5. **Wrong-move text of 60 to 120 characters, right-move text of 100 to 250, story on an optional panel.** chess.com ranges from two words to 700-plus characters on a correct move [V, seg. 8]. Short, concrete, checkable.
6. **Model-game and repertoire formats as a second lane, after rules and tactics.** Players learn openings from "play the winner's side from the critical moment" lessons (Smyslov, Mamedyarov, Ruy Lopez, seg. 13) [V]; for Scholarmate this fits the Openings course and the Games review, with scope stated in the message (item 1) [I].
7. **A lesson ending that is ours to improve (see "Do better").** What chess.com does right: one clear Finish, a Learn page with four buttons [V, seg. 12]. Keep the one-button simplicity.

## Avoid (defects seen repeatedly; each needs a validator or lint)

1. **Grading by the author's move, not by the position.** The engine's own first move is graded red (Mamedyarov L1: Nxd6 +0.32 red, accepted Nxf7 +0.23), a winning move rejected (Step Up in Tactics, +10.8, seg. 5-8), a move tied for first demoted to amber (Smyslov: Bh2+ and Rd3, both +5.65) [V]. Rule [I]: accepted set = every move within about 0.5 pawn of the top and above the item's win threshold; show the course move as "the game's choice".
2. **Words that disagree with numbers, and a state that disagrees with the words.** "Black is OK" at +0.97 for White (My Best Game); "White has a winning attack" at -1.9 (correct, Beat a GM); "playable", "good chances" shown in a red box (Expect the Unexpected, Trompowsky); "stronger move" for gaps of 0.1 and 8 pawns alike (Trompowsky, Carlsen vs Nepomniachtchi) [V]. One margin field should drive state and sentence; add a lint for strong adjectives against the margin [I].
3. **Answers that depend on search depth or are flat.** Dominate Your Opponent's Pieces (first at depth 14, fourth at 18), Expect the Unexpected (second at 14, first at 20), Tal's Best Endgames (seven moves at +0.43) [V]. Validator: escalate depth when the answer is not the top move; below a 0.3-pawn spread, do not issue a verdict, say "both are playable" [I].
4. **Titles that spoil or mislead, and names that disagree.** "Queen Moves" tells you the answer is a queen move; "Bxh7" as a lesson title is a trap (the first move is Ne5, Bxh7+ loses five pawns); Greco Gambit vs a "moller-gambit" slug [V, seg. 13]. Unlabeled titles on test items; one canonical id per line.
5. **Filler and typos in feedback.** "Keep it up", "What's next?" carry nothing; "Balck" in Modern Chess Masterpieces [V]. A lint on learner-facing strings is cheap.
6. **A ending with no data.** Lesson Complete modal, Finish, then /learn with a suggestion that does not depend on the course just played; no score, mistakes or review item [V, seg. 12]. Confirmed for a five-challenge lesson (Tatev, seg. 14): the same modal after the last move, bar full, nothing else.
7. **Principles applied where they do not fit, and mate claims that need depth.** "Avoid moving the same piece twice" was written under a knight that had not moved (Deep Blue course, seg. 14); a mate in 2 (Ra6! bxa6 b7#) is invisible to a depth-12 engine pass, so `validate.py` must use a mate search or depth 20+ for any "mate in N" claim [V, I].
8. **A progress bar that hides length.** "Challenge 1/1" for a 15-move lesson (Checkmate Grandmaster, Roots) [V, seg. 12-13].

## Do better (where Scholarmate can beat chess.com on its own terms)

1. **Unlabeled testing and a contrast lab.** chess.com labels themes in titles and prompts; Scholarmate tests recognition first and names the theme after. "Trap on purpose" items (the tempting named pattern fails and the check shows why) are the strongest teaching form seen, and the London Bxh7 case is one [V, I].
2. **Machine-verified claims, with margins.** chess.com's texts are authored once and never checked against the engine; Scholarmate's hard rule 1 already prevents most of the Avoid list. Make the validator emit the margin band (equal / slightly better / clearly better / winning) and the sentence from it [I].
3. **A real lesson ending.** What you did (right first try, hints, the one miss in words), what it moves on the map (skill and level), the next item with the reason, a come-back-tomorrow date. All of it generated at build time from item metadata, no live AI (rule 7) [I].
4. **Evidence-graded ratings and faded support.** chess.com shows XP and streak on the path and nothing per skill; Scholarmate's grades (G0 to G3) attach to items and learners, and hint use drives fading [I].
5. **Short early lessons and a fast first game.** chess.com's beginner path asks for much reading and many forced repetitions before a free game (new-player journey, seg. 1-4) [V]; the hook (game within ten minutes) is the product's main bet [I].
6. **Honest length indicators on long linear lessons.** "Move 3 of about 12 in this game" instead of 1/1 [I].
7. **A rights-clean content pipeline.** chess.com's library is instructor-owned; Scholarmate uses facts, original wording and checked positions only (rule 4).

## Caveats
Single-position samples from lesson 1 can misrepresent later lessons (most courses were played to the first challenge only). The engine is a depth-limited proxy for "correct"; claims here that "the engine's top move was graded red" are true of this engine at the stated depth, not of every engine. Retention, motivation and pedagogy claims are inferences until Scholarmate has users and data.
