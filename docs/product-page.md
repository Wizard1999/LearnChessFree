## Why another place to learn chess?

Most chess sites do one of three things well: they show you a lot of videos, they give you a lot of puzzles, or they let you play a lot of games. Scholarmate does something different. It teaches the way good coaches teach: it explains an idea, shows it on a board, makes you try it with nobody telling you which idea to use, and then tells you honestly what you can and cannot do yet.

Three promises set it apart.

**1. Every claim is checked.** When a lesson says a move wins a piece, an engine has played the position out and a computer has checked that claim, not only the first move but what the lesson says about it. When a lesson says an ending is a draw, a tablebase (a complete list of results for positions with few pieces) agrees. Things we cannot check are labelled as opinion.

**2. You are tested on what you can do, not on what you were just told.** Real chess never announces "this is a fork". Our exercises often don't either. You find the move in a position with no label, and the app notes whether you needed a hint.

**3. Your progress is evidence, not a streak.** There are no points to farm and no fake level-ups. Each skill fills as you show it: when it is named, when it is not named, and when you rely on it deeply. A finished lesson and a demonstrated skill are shown separately, because they are different things.

## Everything you would expect, and more

### A real curriculum, not a pile of lessons
- **444 lessons across 206 skills**, from "how the rook moves" to rook endings, attack and defence, and calculation.
- **A recommended path in eight stages**, each ending with something you can do in a real game: Moves and the board, Legal play, First complete games, First wins, Becoming independent, Developing strength, Advanced craft, Specialization.
- **One idea per lesson.** Nothing is taught twice; lessons link to each other instead.
- **Stage checks:** mixed, unlabelled tests at the end of each stage, including positions where the right choice is a quiet move or to decline a tactic.

### Practice that does what it says
- **8,594 exercises**, each tied to a skill and a depth, drawn from checked positions, real master games and openly licensed puzzle collections.
- **Silent mode:** no hints, no theme names, like a real game.
- **Spaced review:** skills come back when you are due to forget them.
- **Contrast lab:** the same position with one feature changed, so you learn what actually matters and what does not.
- **Mastery challenges:** six unlabelled exercises on the skills you are most overdue for.

### Play and analyse
- **Play** against Stockfish at gentle levels (a human-like Maia opponent is rolling out: a free neural network trained on human games that plays like a player of about 1100 to 1900, and makes human mistakes).
- **Analysis board:** a full move tree with variations, comments and annotations, an evaluation bar and graph, whole-game analysis, "learn from your mistakes", a board editor and a time-per-move graph from clock comments.
- **My games:** annotate your own game first, then compare with the engine; the app finds your biggest swings and the patterns across your games.
- **Control map:** switch on the squares each side controls, with x-rays, pins and exchange values, to see why a position is what it is. Using it in a test counts as a hint, so it never inflates your record.

### Openings with reasons
- **An openings explorer** with statistics from **329,379 top-level games** (both players rated 2500 or more) and millions of online games by rating band, each clearly labelled with its source and sample size.
- **18 opening deep dives** (and growing), each focused on plans and structures rather than move memory.
- Repertoire builder and a drill that tests you on your own lines.

### Study from the masters
- **19 famous games** taught move by move, each verified against more than one source and checked by an engine.
- **Culture courses:** history and meta, and playstyles measured from real data.
- **A library of 27 books** in the public domain, readable in the app, with every copyright check recorded.

### Built for every kind of learner
- **A curriculum map**: a radial atlas of every skill, with the next step marked, what each skill builds on and leads to, your progress filling in as you learn, and a search box.
- **Read aloud** for lessons, a keyboard-operable board, screen-reader announcements, text size and contrast options, and a phone-friendly layout.
- **Zero setup:** open the page and press Start. No placement test is required, but you can take one.

## What makes it different, in more detail

### We show it, then we say it
A paragraph full of notation is hard to follow if you cannot see the board in your head. Our lessons put every line of moves on a board with one sentence per move, and keep the prose for ideas: why a move works, what it prepares, when it fails. (This is a standard we hold ourselves to; the longer, deeper lessons are being rebuilt to it now, starting with the Sicilian Dragon and the endgame and tactics basics.)

### Honest about what is proven and what is opinion
Every item in the app carries a label for how it was checked: a rules check, an engine check at a stated depth, a tablebase, our own statistics with the sample size, or a named reviewer. Where a lesson offers a rule of thumb, it says "rule of thumb" and says when it fails. Where we are speculating, we say so.

### A tutor that fades itself out
Hints and named themes are scaffolding. As your evidence grows, the support fades: first the label goes, then the hints, then the prompts. The goal is a player who can think without us.

### Learning how to learn
The curriculum includes a short strand on self-direction: noticing what you did not understand, explaining it, diagnosing your own mistakes, planning a training block, testing it fairly and changing it when the evidence says so. By the end you can run your own improvement, and teach the next person.

### Free, and staying free
Scholarmate is a free and open project, licensed GPL-3.0-or-later. Nothing here is a trial, a teaser or a premium tier. It runs on the learner's own device: the lessons, the engine, the practice and your progress all live in your browser. There is no account to make and no data to sell. You can export your progress as a file and take it anywhere.

## A tour of the first hour

1. Press **Start**. The first lesson opens at once: the board and its square names.
2. You learn how each piece moves in the order that makes sense: rook, bishop, queen, knight, pawn, king.
3. You meet check and checkmate before castling, because castling depends on check.
4. By the end of the first stage you can play a legal game and tell check, checkmate and stalemate apart.
5. The next stage adds simple opening habits, looking back at one unsure moment, and your first safety scan.

Every step uses boards you can move, a question that asks you to predict before you see, and a quiet note of how sure you were.

## Questions

**Is it really free?** Yes. There is no paid tier and no plan to add one.

**Do I need an account?** No. Your progress is saved on your device. You can export it and import it elsewhere.

**Is it only a beginner site?** No. The curriculum runs from the rules to calculation, strategy, endgames and opening study, and it is being deepened lesson by lesson.

**How accurate is it?** Chess positions and claims are checked by an engine or a tablebase at a stated depth, and we say when something is a rule of thumb or an opinion. The lessons are still waiting for a strong human player to review them: all of them are marked that way, and we show it.

**Where does the data come from?** The engine is Stockfish. Endgame results come from tablebases. Statistics come from publicly available game databases, including top-level games and online games, with each number labelled with its sample. Puzzles come from openly licensed collections. Books are limited to public-domain texts that passed a copyright check.

**Can I use it on a phone?** Yes. The map and the lessons work on a phone, with pinch zoom on the map.

**Who made it?** Scholarmate was created by Andrew Valentine, with an AI assistant doing much of the authoring and checking under his direction. It is a project, not a company.
