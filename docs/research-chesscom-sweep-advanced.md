## Master Attack and Defense (Chess.com Team, 9 lessons, badges A M, Attacking; lessons: defensive trading, breaking through the castle (advanced), when to attack, seizing the moment, prophylactic thinking 101, defensive sacrifice, killing the target, reinforcing your attack and defence, attacking the vulnerable) [V]
- L1 "Defensive Trading", 3 challenges. The bubble is a 360-character paragraph that states the principle ("a well-timed trade can remove the danger") and describes the position's problems (exposed king, overextended, active queen on c4, weak pawns e4 and c2) and then asks "How can the problems be solved?". C1 `3r2k1/R1p1b1pp/5p2/8/2qBPP2/8/2P2Q1P/6K1 w`: engine c3 -0.64 best, Be3 -4.55, h3 -5.75, Rxc7 -5.94 [V]. c3 got "correct: White protects the bishop without allowing the Black rook to enter. But this move also does something else..." i.e. the feedback sets up the next challenge instead of closing the idea. The second challenge starts straight away; its hint (one press) draws an arrow Qf2-a2 (a queen trade offer, the lesson's title idea) with the word "Try".
- Pattern: positions where the engine score is negative for the learner's side, defended to equality: a "defence" strand in which "best" means "least bad"; the lesson's success claim is wording ("solves the problems"), not an eval. Idea [I]: Scholarmate defence items can use "stay within 0.6 of best" as the machine-checkable claim.

## The Technique of Calculation (Chess.com Team, 10 lessons in a series of themed parts: Bronstein Strikes (4 parts), A Brilliant Deflection (4 parts), Exploiting the Dark Squares ...; badges A M, Tactics) [V]
- L1 "Bronstein Strikes! Part 1", 5 challenges, a real game (Olafsson-Bronstein, Moscow 1959). Intro 470 characters: states the problem, notes the "disconnected" white pieces, and ends "can you find it?" (no theme name). C1 `2r3k1/1b4pp/1p1r3q/2p1np2/2P1pP2/B1QnP3/P1N1B1PP/3R1RK1 b`: engine Rg6 +3.93 best, Ng4 +2.27, Nf7 +1.16, Nd7 +1.01 [V]. Ng4 (also clearly good for Black) was "incorrect: you are looking in the right direction, but this move does not win the game": a reason that is honest about direction but overstates (engine +2.3). Rg6 correct, followed by a coach paragraph that models the calculation process: "every time you consider a sacrifice and take the game into forced lines, take care of the most forced line first; you should also have calculated 2.Kh1, 2.Ne1 and 2.Bxd3; you can check your calculation..." and then continues.
- This is the strongest "teach how to calculate" text seen: it names the candidate list the learner should have checked. A direct model for Scholarmate's calculation habit item: after a right move show the checklist of opponent replies to calculate (forcing line first, then the quiet ones). Defect [I]: grading "does not win the game" for a +2.3 move with no further explanation.

## Pawn Structures That Win (Aman Hambleton, 5 lessons, 25 challenges, 45 min, badges A M, Strategy; lessons: the Maroczy Bind, the f3/e4 chain, Sicilian, the e3 Maroczy, doubled f-pawns) [V]
- L1 "The Maroczy Bind", 5 challenges, no explanatory paragraph: the bubble only says "White to Move" (the prompt text did not appear in two reads, so this lesson may simply have no prompt [V as observed]). C1 `r2q1rk1/1p2ppbp/3p1np1/p2b4/2PBP3/8/PP3PPP/R2QRBK1 w` (Black has just put a bishop on d5): engine exd5 +0.83 best, cxd5 +0.15, Bxf6 -0.56 [V]. cxd5 (playable, 0.7 worse) was "an alternative: this is playable, but there is a better capture, try to find it", so the alternative tier tracks a real eval gap. exd5: "Nice move! - Observation [I]: the lesson label is the structure; the question is an ordinary capture choice. The structure idea is carried by the sequence (useful trades), not stated in the prompt, which is unlabeled testing, but with no explanation a learner who does not know the Bind learns nothing about why.

## Opposite-Colored Bishops (GM Dejan Bojkov, 10 lessons; titles: Good Defense, Bad Bishop, Bad Bishop Mating Threats, Weak Squares, Breakthrough 1 and 2, One More Time With Feeling, Room for the King, Frozen Pieces, The Move of the Century; badges A M, Endgames) [V]
- L1 "Good Defense", 6 challenges, positions from real endgames. C1 `8/8/1b6/4p3/3k1p2/5B2/4K3/8 w` (opposite-coloured bishops, Black two pawns up, no prompt text): engine Bg2 -1.30, Bh1 -1.32, Bc6 -3.39, Ba8 -3.87 [V] (White is struggling; the lesson asks for the best defence). Wrong Bc6: "56.Bc6? e4 followed by 57...f3+ wins for Black. She will transfer her king to f4 and advance the e-pawn. When the pawns reach the third rank there is no salvation for the defender!": a concrete refutation line in notation, a plan and a general rule. Right Bg2: "the right spot! From here it controls f3 and hits the e4 pawn, so the pawns cannot move further and the black king cannot get active."
- Feedback quality is the best of the Advanced courses seen: line, plan and principle in three sentences (about 270 characters). Idea [I]: for defence and endgame items the wrong-move text can follow "the move, the refutation line, the plan, the rule", a four-part template for Scholarmate's contrast items.

## Premature Attacks on the King (Chess.com Team, 15 lessons, badges A M, Attacking; lessons include Knights Are Strong Defensive Pieces, Attack on h2, A Mysterious Defensive Rook Move, Don't Panic, An Unexpected Dangerous Diagonal, A Premature Sacrifice on h3, The Best Defense Is Sometimes Indirect) [V]
- Defence-of-sacrifices course. L1 has 3 challenges and no prompt text beyond "Black to Move" (the intro line "That can't be sound!" is on the course page). C1 `r1bq1rk1/1p2nppB/p1nb4/3p4/1PpP4/P1P1BN1P/3N1PP1/R2Q1RK1 b` (White has just sacrificed Bxh7+): engine Kxh7 +4.52, Kh8 -2.17 [V]. Wrong Kh8: "Ok, ok. Try again!": the only feedback so far that speaks to the learner's fear and also gives the arithmetic. Right Kxh7: "That's right! Way to go!!!" The lesson titles name the defensive resource in the title ("Don't Panic", "Knights Are Strong Defensive Pieces"), so the first position teaches the habit "take the piece, then defend".

## Cross-course patterns (Advanced level, segment 6)
1. Feedback tiers (confirmed across 13 courses): correct / "is an alternative" (playable, better exists) / "is incorrect". The alternative tier tracks a real eval gap in Play the Italian, Pawn Structures That Win, When Computers Go Wrong, Play Like Levy; it is absent or inconsistent in the 2018 Team courses where engine-sound moves are "incorrect" (Wolff Back Rank Nf5 +5.0, Technique of Calculation Ng4 +2.3).
2. Wrong-move text quality scale, best to worst: (a) refutation line + plan + rule (Opposite-Colored Bishops), (b) names the opponent's best reply or the cost (Defending Difficult Positions "Black wins material", Recognizing the Opponent's Moves "Ne7 and still has chances", Do or Die with Rook Endings), (c) restates the goal ("that won't win material"), (d) bare "incorrect" (Puzzle Rush, Illingworth).
3. Hint ladders are 2 to 3 presses: "Hint" label, then piece highlight, then arrow, sometimes with a one-line text (Hikaru Plays to Win has all three). A single press that jumps straight to the arrow also exists (Master Attack and Defense).
4. Text volume: master-annotated courses (Technique of Calculation, Master Attack and Defense, Do or Die with Rook Endings, Pawn Play) open with a 260 to 750 character paragraph; Team tactics series (Wolff, Recognizing the Opponent's Moves, Premature Attacks, Pawn Structures That Win) often show no prompt at all; the Play Like and Hikaru series use one line.
5. Handoffs: the right move usually continues the same line with a new question ("How should you start a strong kingside attack?"), so a challenge is a short sequence, not a single puzzle ("Challenge 1/1" in Play Like is a whole game).
6. Course/lesson naming: concept-named lessons (Knight Endgames, Opposite-Colored Bishops, Pawn Play) are far easier to navigate than numbered ones (Wolff series, Recognizing the Opponent's Moves "lesson-N-13").
7. Position grading follows the author's concept more than the engine: Pawn Play (e5 not in the top four), Hikaru Plays to Win (Nd2 second best, near tie), Play Like Levy (the game move g6 outside the top four). None says that the engine disagrees.

### Don't Let Them Castle (IM Levy Rozman, 4 lessons, 20 challenges, 44 min, M badge, Strategy; lessons: Be a Pest, How to Maintain the Initiative, Don't Be Afraid to Sacrifice, When Things Go Wrong) [V]
- L1 C1 `r1b1k2r/bpppqppp/p1n2n2/8/2BPP3/P1N4P/3N1PP1/R1BQ1RK1 w` (Black has not castled): engine e5 +4.13, Nf3 +3.25, Nd5 +2.09, Nb3 +1.59 [V]. Nf3 (a clearly good move) was "an alternative: White has a good position, but there is a better and more forcing option to prevent castling, try to find it": states the lesson's criterion (forcing, prevents castling). e5: "Great move! The only move to save the f6 knight is Ng8, which will make it very difficult for Black to castle. White has a powerful attack." The claim "the only move" is stronger than the engine supports in general (Black can also give up the knight for pawns), unchecked [I].
- Teaching idea [I]: one practical aim per course ("stop the king from castling") makes the candidate-move filter explicit ("is it forcing? does it prevent castling?"); a nice model for a Scholarmate "goal-first" lesson with a one-sentence criterion.

### Hall of Fame: The 50 Greatest Chess Players of All Time (GM Jan Gustafsson, 50 lessons, 114 challenges, 623 min, M badge, Master Games; lessons run 50 down to 1, one lesson per player, e.g. "50: Aron Nimzowitsch") [V]
- Format: biography-and-game course where each player's lesson is 2 to 3 challenges taken from one of his games. Lesson 50 Nimzowitsch C1 `r1b2rk1/p2qnppp/1p1p1n2/2p5/2PPpP2/1NP1P2P/P3B1P1/R1B1QRK1 b` "How did Nimzowitsch restrict the white kingside?" Engine: h5 +1.20, h6 +0.38, Ba6 +0.36, a5 +0.33 [V] (a restricting pawn advance on the side the prompt names). a5 (0.9 worse) was "incorrect: there is a better option, look for a useful pawn advance"; h5: "Good start! - The player-per-lesson structure doubles as a history/culture strand with chess content per player, in the order of a countdown; the prompts hint the concept (restrict) like Pawn Structures That Win. Idea [I]: history lessons can be anchored to a playable position of the player's most characteristic idea (Nimzowitsch: restriction/prophylaxis), the only cheap way to keep a history course chess-relevant.

- A chess-history strand told by engine games, one game per lesson, 5 challenges. L1 "Deep Thought" C1 `rn1qkb1r/ppp1pppp/5n2/8/2PP2b1/2N2N2/PP3PPP/R1BQKB1R b` (the learner plays the human side; board rotated). ### Roots of Positional Understanding (IM Jeremy Silman, 286 lessons, badges I A M, Strategy; lesson slugs like "thematic-opening-idea", "crossing-the-opponents-plans", "expanding-your-possibilities", "pieces-working-together", "plugging-the-holes", plus named games such as Fischer-Gheorghiu 1970 and youth-festival games) [V]
- Largest course in the library (286 lessons). L1 "Thematic Opening Idea" has one challenge (1/1) with a 530-character prompt that also tells the move sequence so far ("1.e4 d6 2.d4 Nf6 3.Nc3 g6 4.Bc4 Bg7 5.Nf3 0-0 6.h3") and says the idea occurs "from many different openings". C1 `rnbq1rk1/ppp1ppbp/3p1np1/8/2BPP3/2N2N1P/PPP2PP1/R1BQK2R b`: engine Nxe4 -0.09, a6 -0.31, c6 -0.31, Nc6 -0.37 [V] (Nxe4 equalises, as the lesson says). Wrong Nc6: a long text that first praises the move ("a logical move hitting d4 and e5 and preparing ...e5"), lists White's replies (2.e5!?, 2.d5, 2.0-0) and says the problem is that "you missed a more effective alternative" (text longer than I read; truncated by my read at 300 characters). Right Nxe4: closes the lesson immediately ("Lesson Complete! Way to go!") with a paragraph naming the idea ("a centre-destroying combination, a common theme, to be imprinted on your brain") and the line 2.Bxf7+ Rxf7 3.Nxe4 h6 with the two bishops.
- Observations [I]: (1) a lesson can be a single move plus a long annotated paragraph, so one lesson is about 2 minutes; (2) the pattern "praise the wrong move's logic, then say what it misses" is the most respectful wrong-move text seen and also the longest; (3) the lesson explanation states a general claim ("equality by trading pieces and easing the cramp") that the engine confirms at depth 14 [V].

### Yusupov On Aronian: Fighting For The Initiative With Black (GM Artur Yusupov, 3 lessons, 15 challenges, 166 min, M badge, Strategy; lessons are three games: Giri-Aronian, Carlsen-Aronian, So-Aronian) [V]
- 166 minutes for 15 challenges: the heaviest time-per-challenge ratio seen, because the coach's commentary is long (video-like) around five challenges per game. L1 C1 `r1bq1rk1/1p1nbpp1/2p1p2p/p2p4/P1PPnB2/1Q3NP1/1P1NPPBP/R1R3K1 b` (Black to move, a quiet position, no prompt text read). Engine: g5 +0.19, Nf6 -0.13, f5 -0.23, Bb4 -0.24 [V], all within 0.45, so the grader again follows the author's idea (the pawn lunge g5 supports the e4 knight). Nf6 (a normal move, 0.3 worse) got "an alternative: this is OK, but let's find a strong threat instead". g5 got "Great move! How should you support your centralized knight?" and the game continues.
- Idea [I]: in a near-equal position the hint text "find a strong threat" sets the criterion; Scholarmate's accepted-alternatives band could say "any move within 0.5 is OK, the lesson's move is the one with a threat". Defect [I]: positions where five moves are within 0.45 are weak ground for single-answer grading; the strength is the commentary, not the grading.
