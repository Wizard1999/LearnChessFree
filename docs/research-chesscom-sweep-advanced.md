## Cross-course patterns (Advanced level, segment 6)
1. Feedback tiers (confirmed across 13 courses): correct / "is an alternative" (playable, better exists) / "is incorrect". The alternative tier tracks a real eval gap in Play the Italian, Pawn Structures That Win, When Computers Go Wrong, Play Like Levy; it is absent or inconsistent in the 2018 Team courses where engine-sound moves are "incorrect" (Wolff Back Rank Nf5 +5.0, Technique of Calculation Ng4 +2.3).
2. Wrong-move text quality scale, best to worst: (a) refutation line + plan + rule (Opposite-Colored Bishops), (b) names the opponent's best reply or the cost (Defending Difficult Positions "Black wins material", Recognizing the Opponent's Moves "Ne7 and still has chances", Do or Die with Rook Endings), (c) restates the goal ("that won't win material"), (d) bare "incorrect" (Puzzle Rush, Illingworth).
3. Hint ladders are 2 to 3 presses: "Hint" label, then piece highlight, then arrow, sometimes with a one-line text (Hikaru Plays to Win has all three). A single press that jumps straight to the arrow also exists (Master Attack and Defense).
4. Text volume: master-annotated courses (Technique of Calculation, Master Attack and Defense, Do or Die with Rook Endings, Pawn Play) open with a 260 to 750 character paragraph; Team tactics series (Wolff, Recognizing the Opponent's Moves, Premature Attacks, Pawn Structures That Win) often show no prompt at all; the Play Like and Hikaru series use one line.
5. Handoffs: the right move usually continues the same line with a new question ("How should you start a strong kingside attack?"), so a challenge is a short sequence, not a single puzzle ("Challenge 1/1" in Play Like is a whole game).
6. Course/lesson naming: concept-named lessons (Knight Endgames, Opposite-Colored Bishops, Pawn Play) are far easier to navigate than numbered ones (Wolff series, Recognizing the Opponent's Moves "lesson-N-13").
7. Position grading follows the author's concept more than the engine: Pawn Play (e5 not in the top four), Hikaru Plays to Win (Nd2 second best, near tie), Play Like Levy (the game move g6 outside the top four). None says that the engine disagrees.
