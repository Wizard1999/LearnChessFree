# Missions: progress tracking inspired by Khan Academy's "World of Math"

Andrew asked for lesson progress tracking like the old Khan Academy mission dashboards (the "World of Math"): a donut of mission progress, a level for every skill, small blocks that darken as you advance, a "skills up next" list and the Mastery Challenge. This note records what the system was, what people said about it, what I could and could not see, and how it maps onto this project's evidence model. Evidence labels: **[V]** read or seen here, **[S]** secondary, **[I]** my inference.

## What it was [V unless marked]

- Missions (retired June 2020, per staff; the introduction date is not in the pages I read) were per-grade or per-course dashboards. The "World of Math" was the largest: about 1,500 skills laid out in one place, grouped into missions from early math to calculus, each mission split into sections of skills.
- Five skill levels: not started, practiced, level one, level two, mastered. Blocks were shaded from light to dark blue by level. How the mission percentage was computed is not stated in the sources I read; the average-level rule used here is my own choice **[I]**.
- **Mastery Challenge**: available every 16 hours when practiced skills existed; it could raise a skill by one level, lower it by one, or leave it, and it also tested skills already marked mastered, to check they were still known. **Booster Challenge**: practice with no penalty. **Mission Warm-Up**: a test of what you might already know, so you could test out; the fan wiki describes it as without penalty, with qualifications I did not reproduce, so the "no penalty" rule here is stated with its own condition (see below) **[I]**.
- A recommendation stream ("skills up next") picked skills based on what you had done and what you still needed; you could add skills by clicking blocks and **remove** any suggestion.
- Points, badges ("Geek of the week: mastery" for five Mastery Challenges in a week) and an end-of-mission celebration screen.
- Staff's reasons for removal (June 2020): the old code base was hard to maintain; teachers could not assign missions and track them; it was unclear to teachers how mastery was evaluated; missions were not integrated with the course library; they covered only math and some computing.
- The successor, Course Mastery: the 2018 Unit Mastery **beta** post (posted by a community account in the style of a Khan Academy statement; I could not confirm the author) lists levels Familiar, Proficient, Mastered with 50, 80 and 100 points. The help-centre article describes the new Mastery Challenge of **6 questions across 3 skills, 2 per skill**: both right moves a skill up, both wrong moves it down, one each keeps it; it unlocks after Familiar on 3 skills, Proficient on 1, and 12 hours since the last one, and chooses skills by time since last review and level (spaced review). A challenge could move a level by two.

## What people said [S: user posts, not studies]

Praised: seeing the whole of math on one screen and watching it darken ("the biggest draw"); a tech-tree feel where prerequisites were visible; mastery in small sets so one slip did not erase progress, compared with unit tests of nine or more questions that needed perfection; recommendations that "felt correct"; test-out through the warm-up and challenges; a less formal feel than quizzes and unit tests.
Criticised or complicated: in the World of Math community thread, users complained about losing mastery after an error on a test; in the 2018 successor beta people disliked missing progress counters and being prompted for points (one beta user liked the pressure); staff said the rules for reaching mastery were opaque to teachers. Several users asked for it back after removal; staff said a replacement is on a wish list. (An earlier version of this note also reported a teacher's complaint about whole-grade challenges; I could not find that in the pages cited, so it is removed.)

## Our version

| Khan Academy idea | Here | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Mission per course | A **mission per strand** (S1 to S11); large strands split by tier band | We already have 11 strands and 204 nodes |
| Block per skill | One block per **skill node**; shading by level; a corner mark when its lesson has been finished | Shows both "viewed" and "level" as asked |
| Five levels | Not started, **Seen** (lesson or any answer), **Level one** (D1 reliable), **Level two** (D2 reliable), **Mastered** (the deepest depth with exercises reliable and not due for a refresher). A depth with no exercises counts as met when a deeper depth that has exercises is reliable. A depth whose exercises are all cued (cue 1-2) can be reliable on cued evidence, and the page says so | Levels come from the same evidence as the rest of the app: first attempts, cue weights, "reliable" needs unlabelled evidence where unlabelled exercises exist. No separate counters to drift. Limits: a repeated exercise adds a quarter of the evidence; "Mastered" does not yet require evidence from two different days, and ignores depth D4 (in play) **[I]** |
| Mission % | Average level across the mission's nodes, **counting only what the learner has shown**. Skills assumed from a placement or warm-up are shown hatched and as a lighter part of the ring, not as progress | The original's rule is unknown **[I]**; the arithmetic is shown on the page. Nodes with no exercises can reach Seen at most, so the ring cannot fill, and the page says so |
| Mastery Challenge | **6 questions, 3 skills, 2 each**, unlabelled, fresh, no hints, from two different positions per skill; the most overdue skills first and at most one mastered skill, ranked last (a retention check); unlocks after 3 skills with answered exercises and 12 hours (the gap is copied from Khan Academy's rule; its reason, spaced review, is the same, but we do not use their points or 16-hour clock); results are recorded as ordinary first attempts (source "challenge") and the level is recomputed from evidence; the page says what moved and why | Keeps what people valued (small, spaced, can raise or lower, checks retention) and makes the rule readable, which staff said was the weakness |
| Mission Warm-Up | A per-strand test-out: about ten unlabelled questions, a random sample of each tier's skills (two or three per tier), stops early after two weak tiers in a row; **a tier needs at least two questions, and two of three right (66%), to raise the frontier**; skills below the frontier are **assumed**, shown separately, left out of Continue, and replaced by evidence as soon as a miss or a weak record appears. A wrong answer on a skill never practised is not held against the learner; after real practice every answer counts. Retaking is allowed | Test out without penalty. The bar is far below the app's own "reliable" rule, which is why assumed skills are not counted as progress |
| Booster | Ordinary Practice. Misses there count as evidence, like everywhere else | Already exists. (An earlier version of this table said practice is "never penalised"; that was wrong) |
| "Skills up next" with Remove | Same, from prerequisites met and lowest level first; Remove hides a suggestion on this device | Learner control; small |
| Points, energy points, badges, streaks | **Not now** | The doctrine (`docs/design-direction.md`) asks for no shame mechanics; points need a justification we do not have yet |

A level can go down only through new evidence (a missed exercise, a challenge) or time: a Mastered skill drops to Level two when its refresher becomes due (21 days after the last unlabelled answer). Nothing else is deducted for time away. The page states that rule in plain words.

## What the first review found, and what changed (25 October 2026)
An adversarial review of the first build (three reviewers; the findings below were confirmed by repro or by reading the code) led to these fixes: the warm-up plan is frozen when it starts (it used to be re-planned after every answer, so questions repeated and were skipped); challenge ranking puts mastered skills last (the sign was inverted); depths with only cued exercises can now be reliable (140 of 332 depths with exercises had no unlabelled item, so most lessons sat behind prerequisites that could never be met); a prerequisite depth with no exercises is met by finishing the skill's lesson; assumed skills no longer count as progress and are not overwritten by one right answer; repeats of one exercise count a quarter; the refresher clock ignores cue-1 lesson answers; challenges are silent, so every answer counts as it stands; a finished lesson is no longer marked unfinished when re-read; retaking the placement after a skip clears the skip; wording that overstated what the code does was corrected. Not changed: the 12-hour gap and "Mastered" evidence rules above are still first guesses.
