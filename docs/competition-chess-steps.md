## What it is
A graded Dutch course for children and adults, developed in 1987 by Rob Brunia (a pedagogue) and IM Cor van Wijgerden, and the official method of the Dutch federation [S: lichess blog by a trainer, via search snippet, page itself returned 403: https://lichess.org/@/HanSchut/blog/the-steps-method-for-learning-and-teaching-chess/L74I4Ihq]. Six steps run from the rules to roughly 2100 strength. The official English site says the top is about "top 700 of the Netherlands" and that schools and clubs in several countries use it [S: https://www.stappenmethode.nl/en/]. A German shop claims over 400,000 learners; that is a vendor claim [S: https://chess-tigers.de/en/blogs/news/stappenmethode, search snippet]. Dutch editions are titled "Lekker schaken", edited by Hans Böhm [S: KNSB-related search results].

Rating bands per step (as stated by one site, not independently checked, and one commenter says they need revising after FIDE's 2024 rating adjustment): Step 1 to 800, Step 2 to 1400, Step 3 to 1600, Step 4 to 1750, Step 5 to 1900, Step 6 to 2100 [S: https://www.chess-steps.com/Producten/978-90-77275-29-0-workbook-step-2.php].

## How it teaches (mechanics)
- **Two books per step:** a trainer manual of ready-made lessons (224 to 256 pages; Step 4 has 17 base lessons and 11 "plus" lessons, Step 5 has 16 and 10, Step 3 has 17 and 14) and a thin student workbook of 56 pages [S: https://www.stappenmethode.nl/en/step4.php, /step5.php, /step3.php, /step1.php, /step2.php]. The pairing is meant to create "a school-like situation" [S: chess-steps.com page above]. The manual is written so the trainer's own chess strength matters little [S: publisher claim, via search].
- **Explanation is short, exercises are the bulk.** Workbook Step 1 holds about 486 exercises and Step 2 about 526 exercises plus 9 "reminders" (one-page summaries of what the trainer taught); Step 4 about 631 [S: step pages]. So roughly 500 to 630 positions per step in a 56-page book, about 9 to 10 per page [I: arithmetic from those counts]. Full teaching happens orally from the manual; the workbook is the practice.
- **Five companion workbook types per step** [S: https://www.stappenmethode.nl/en/new-workbooks.php]: Basic (matches lessons), Extra (more of the same; first half same subjects, second half mixed without labels, resembling game positions; later steps end with 10 to 26 pages of tests), Plus (new or neglected topics, depth), Thinking ahead (calculation and blindfold-style tasks, Steps 2 and 3 only), Mix (about 720 unlabelled exercises drawn from the step and its plus books; scoring 80% is described as the ultimate test of mastering a step). Each step has four or five books, Step 6 two.
- **Labelled then unlabelled:** themes are first drilled with the theme known, then the Extra and Mix books remove the label so learners must recognise the pattern. The site says the process and feedback on errors matter as much as the answer [S: new-workbooks page].
- **Theme sequence** [S: step pages]: Step 1 rules, first skills, mating late; Step 2 attack techniques and winning material, tactics before position ("all games are decided by tactics" at this level), positions noticed from pupils' own games; Step 3 tactics and defending against them, trapping pieces, first pawn endings, thinking ahead; Step 4 preparatory moves (luring, removing the defender, chasing, aiming, clearance), solutions about 2.5 moves deep, modest positional work, weak pawns; Step 5 tactics still central plus pawn structure, seventh rank, strong squares, open files, endgame plans, defence; it is the last group manual, often used for self-study. Search methods per tactical theme ("building blocks") are taught [S: chess-steps.com].
- **Exams and diplomas:** clubs report that each step comes with a homework book, an exam and an official KNSB diploma [S: https://www.kidsproof.nl/denbosch/?p=34281]. Exams are scheduled several times a season, candidates preselected by clubs, with regional exam days [S: KNSB sheet, summary only]. Number of questions and pass mark: **not found**.
- **Digital:** a trainer on Lichess complained the digital version stops at Step 3 [S: Lichess forum snippet]. Current state of any online course: not established.

## Why it works [H]
1. High-volume, narrow-theme repetition builds pattern recognition, the thing most amateurs lack at low levels [H, consistent with chunking accounts].
2. The step gate (diploma, 80% mix score) gives a visible finish line and prevents skipping to hard material [H].
3. Splitting teacher talk from learner practice means practice time is not eaten by explanation, and every lesson has the same shape, lowering cognitive load [H].
4. Unlabelled mixes at the end of each step force recognition rather than "this is a pin chapter" cueing [H].
5. Theme-by-theme tactics before positional ideas matches how games are actually decided below about 1400 [H].

## Limits
- **Tactics-heavy.** One forum user describes the structure as exercises, exercises, exercises with a tactical focus; a commenter warns too much tactics can come at the cost of positional understanding (general point, not about Steps only) [S: chess.com forum, Substack comment via search]. Positional play is introduced mostly from Step 4 on.
- **Depends on a teacher.** The workbook alone has little prose; one forum user says adults can do Steps 1 and 2 alone but a coach helps with analysing one's own games [S: Lichess forum snippet].
- **No game loop in the product.** Learners play at the club; the method does not process their games [I].
- **Openings and strategy deep dives thin.** Opening work is in Plus books; I found no course-level opening teaching [S/I].
- **Evidence:** no independent trial of effectiveness found; reviews are mostly vendor text. I found no named coach criticising repetitiveness [S: negative result].
- **Paper-based:** feedback is a trainer reading answers; answer sheets are downloadable for trainers [S].

## What we should adopt, adapt or reject
| Area (ours) | Steps does | Decision | Why / how |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lessons | Short oral lesson + one-page reminder | **Adapt** | Keep the reminder idea: a closing "what to remember" card per lesson (5 to 8 lines, not a paragraph). Reject thin teaching: our brief wants 10 to 20 slides with board-shown lines, which Steps leaves to the human trainer |
| Exercises per theme | About 500 to 630 per step, ~10 per page | **Adopt in spirit** | A lesson should feed a pool of many varied positions of one theme, not 3 to 6. Target dozens per theme in the pools, machine-verified, unlabelled variants included |
| Practice pools | Basic, Extra, Plus, Mix as separate books | **Adopt** | Same four roles as pool modes: matched-to-lesson, repetition, depth/new, mixed-unlabelled. Our recycling is scheduled by evidence, which a book cannot do |
| Stage checks | Exam at end of step; 80% on Mix = mastery | **Adopt, adapt** | A mixed unlabelled check with a stated threshold, per stage, with retest later. Do not issue a "diploma"; evidence grade only (rule 6) |
| Path / curriculum | Fixed six-step line by rating band | **Adapt** | We have a prerequisite graph; offer a "recommended order" lane shaped like six steps for people who want a line. Rating bands only as G0 guesses |
| Courses | Step = course | **Adapt** | Add a "thinking ahead / calculation" strand like their Thinking-ahead books (we are weak on visualisation drills) |
| Theme structure | Tactical building blocks with a search method | **Adopt** | Teach a named search routine per tactic (what to look for, in what order), then drill |
| Positional teaching | Late, modest | **Reject the lateness** | Introduce structure and plans earlier, with board-shown examples, since Andrew's level is mid and adults differ from children [H] |
| Games | Not part of the product | **Keep our edge** | Games feed the graph; use game positions as the unlabelled test source, as Extra's second half imitates |
| Adult vs child | Child-centred, trainer-led | **Reject** | Self-directed, no trainer: all explanation must live in the lesson, which is the owner's complaint about shallow lessons |
| Exams as gate | Club-run, human-graded | **Reject gate, adopt visible milestone** | Show stage progress, never block |
