# chess.com sweep: families B (Play Like, Top 10 decade lists, event recaps, deeper lessons) (started 9 Nov 2026, segment 15)

Sign convention for every note in this file [V]: engine scores (eng.py, local Stockfish 19 lite, `tools/engine.py`) are from the side to move, so a positive number means the side to move is better; for a Black-to-move position the note says "Black's view" and a negative number means Black is worse. FENs were read from `game.getFEN()` in the lesson page, never typed by hand. Depth 14 unless stated. Original wording only; short facts, no copied lesson text. Labels: [V] verified here, [S] secondary, [I] inference.

Coverage of this file: the unsampled "Play Like" courses (Jeremy Kane), the Top 10 decade lists, event recaps, then deeper lessons of long courses. Tool notes: (1) the course page can be fetched with `fetch('/lessons/<slug>')` from any chess.com tab and the lesson links parsed from the HTML, so one JS call lists the lessons of 40 courses [V]; (2) a lesson URL can be opened directly and `Start` clicked in-page, no course-page visit needed [V]; (3) the navigation JS and the Start JS must be separate messages (the second is cut off otherwise) except when the navigation is a plain `navigate` call [V].

Gaps found against chess.com [I, from the lesson lists and samples above]:
1. Player portraits: chess.com has about 41 five-game "Play Like X" courses (around 200 games); Scholarmate has 4 style lessons and no per-player course. Cheapest build: a "Meet the players" strand of one lesson per player (3 critical positions of one famous game each, predict-the-move), original commentary, moves checked against two sources. Priority order by name recognition: Morphy (exists in famous-games), Capablanca, Alekhine, Tal, Fischer, Kasparov, Karpov, Carlsen, Polgar, Anand, Kramnik, Petrosian, Spassky, Smyslov, Botvinnik, Steinitz, Lasker, Caruana, Nakamura, Gukesh, Firouzja.
2. Missing modern: Carlsen games (Ernst 2004, Karjakin 2013, Aronian 2017 loss), Firouzja-Zarkovic 2019, Nepomniachtchi-Carlsen 2020, AlphaZero-Stockfish 2017, Ding Liren and Wei Yi brilliancies, Rapport and Duda games. Facts only; commentary original.
3. Pop-culture hook: Play Like Beth Harmon, Chess In The Movies and Queen's Gambit positions (Greco 1620 game, the Hilton Wexler puzzle) are cheap hooks; none in Scholarmate [grep "Beth": 2 files, neither a lesson]. Mate-in-3 puzzle from the series is a clean checkable item (mate in 3 by Kd7; engine mate score at depth 18).
4. Pawn-structure breadth: Hellsten's 30 lessons vs Scholarmate's 9 structure skills. 5. Weak color complexes: `grep` for "color complex" and "colour complex" over `content/` and `docs/` finds nothing outside the research notes; chess.com has a 22-lesson course on it (David Pruess, sampled earlier). Gap.
6. Event recaps (Candidates, Speed Chess Championship, Nations Cup): current-events content; none in Scholarmate; not recommended for v1 (dated) but a "famous tournament games" mini-strand per year could reuse the games.
7. Findings from the five lessons [V]:
1. Error replies come in three grades: generic (does not do X, try again), consequence-naming (the knight will be captured by the pawn on g7), property-naming (the pawn is not protected; find a protected one). Only the third says what to look for next. A legal escape can be marked wrong because a stronger move exists (Getting Out of Check C2, C4), which teaches capture-before-retreat without saying so.
2. The generic reply can be factually wrong (Qxf7+ "does not attack the king"), and stalemate is never named in a lesson whose wrong answers can be stalemates (Checkmate C3, Bc7). A content gap Scholarmate can fill: the stalemate lesson (S1) should follow that near-miss.
3. Rule lessons use real game positions from lesson 13 on (Castling C1 is the Italian after 1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Nf6 3.Bc4 Bc5): the first 30-piece board appears at minute 15 to 18, as a position to answer a question about, not a game to play.
4. A check lesson with only 1 or 2 legal checks per position is a search over a tiny set; it rewards trying every move, and a hint-free rule lesson cannot tell that from understanding.

### C. What Scholarmate should match or beat (against `content/path.json` S0 to S2) [I]

path.json S0 ("Moves and the board", 8 lessons), S1 ("Legal play", 7) and S2 ("First complete games", 22) cover the same rule content as the 2018 course (13 lessons) and the 2021 starter group (12 lessons) plus the first game; ours puts the game first (L-FND-FIRSTGAME-1, then board and pieces).
- Match: time to first legal move (about 20 s from Start to a king move) and the size of the atom (5 short steps, a one-sentence prompt of 10 to 25 words, a retry that returns to the same position).
- Match or beat: first enemy piece (chess.com: minute 9 to 12 in 2018, about 21 in 2021; ours is the opening game) and first mate (minute 14 to 15 in 2018, about 31 in 2021). Beat the way the 2018 mate arrives: it gives the goal away in the prompt; a mate we reach should be found by the learner (a one-move choice with a reason). `tools/check_path_time.py` is the measuring tool.
- Beat: wrong-answer replies. chess.com has three grades and the best, property-naming reply appears in about one challenge in five lessons; our `why_wrong` should always name the missing property and never be wrong (their "does not attack the king" for a check is false). - Beat: toy positions until minute 15 to 18. Their first 30-piece position is a Castling question at minute 15 to 18; ours is a real game at minute 3, so castling is taught at the move the game needs it (D56).
- Do not copy: the order (king, each piece, rules last, castling last), five steps for every lesson whatever it teaches (1 to 22 minutes for the same step count shows the minute figure is not a design unit), and lessons whose only difficulty is trying every legal move (Check: 1 to 2 candidates).
