# chess.com sweep: families B (Play Like, Top 10 decade lists, event recaps, deeper lessons) (started 9 Nov 2026, segment 15)

Sign convention for every note in this file [V]: engine scores (eng.py, local Stockfish 19 lite, `tools/engine.py`) are from the side to move, so a positive number means the side to move is better; for a Black-to-move position the note says "Black's view" and a negative number means Black is worse. FENs were read from `game.getFEN()` in the lesson page, never typed by hand. Depth 14 unless stated. Original wording only; short facts, no copied lesson text. Labels: [V] verified here, [S] secondary, [I] inference.

Coverage of this file: the unsampled "Play Like" courses (Jeremy Kane), the Top 10 decade lists, event recaps, then deeper lessons of long courses. Tool notes: (1) the course page can be fetched with `fetch('/lessons/<slug>')` from any chess.com tab and the lesson links parsed from the HTML, so one JS call lists the lessons of 40 courses [V]; (2) a lesson URL can be opened directly and `Start` clicked in-page, no course-page visit needed [V]; (3) the navigation JS and the Start JS must be separate messages (the second is cut off otherwise) except when the navigation is a plain `navigate` call [V].

## Gaps for Scholarmate (grep of `content/` and `docs/`, 9 Nov 2026) [V for what exists, I for judgement]

What Scholarmate already has [V]: `content/courses/famous-games.json` with 19 annotated games (Opera, Immortal, Evergreen, Morphy-Paulsen, Steinitz-von Bardeleben, Lasker-Bauer, Lasker-Capablanca, Capablanca-Marshall, Botvinnik-Capablanca, Nimzowitsch-Samisch, Reti-Tartakower, Rubinstein, Fischer-Spassky 6, Petrosian-Spassky 10, Tal-Botvinnik 6, Karpov-Kasparov 24, Kasparov-Topalov, Short-Timman, Game of the Century); `playstyles.json` with 4 lessons (Anand-Kramnik, Carlsen-Nakamura, Caruana-Aronian, "what numbers say") built on measured statistics; 9 POS.STRUCTURE skills (Carlsbad, chain, doubled, hanging, hedgehog, IQP, KID, Maroczy) plus OPN.STRUCT; GAME.* skills for patterns and history. So the structure of the Play Like idea (study the games of one player) is only half there.

Gaps found against chess.com [I, from the lesson lists and samples above]:
1. Player portraits: chess.com has about 41 five-game "Play Like X" courses (around 200 games); Scholarmate has 4 style lessons and no per-player course. Cheapest build: a "Meet the players" strand of one lesson per player (3 critical positions of one famous game each, predict-the-move), original commentary, moves checked against two sources. Priority order by name recognition: Morphy (exists in famous-games), Capablanca, Alekhine, Tal, Fischer, Kasparov, Karpov, Carlsen, Polgar, Anand, Kramnik, Petrosian, Spassky, Smyslov, Botvinnik, Steinitz, Lasker, Caruana, Nakamura, Gukesh, Firouzja.
2. Games by era: the Top 10 decade lists (60 games, 1950s to 2010s) and the World Championship match courses (Steinitz years to Carlsen years, about 55 match games) are an historical spine; Scholarmate has GAME.HISTORY as a skill but only 19 games, and the latest is Kasparov-Topalov 1999. Missing modern: Carlsen games (Ernst 2004, Karjakin 2013, Aronian 2017 loss), Firouzja-Zarkovic 2019, Nepomniachtchi-Carlsen 2020, AlphaZero-Stockfish 2017, Ding Liren and Wei Yi brilliancies, Rapport and Duda games. Facts only; commentary original.
3. Pop-culture hook: Play Like Beth Harmon, Chess In The Movies and Queen's Gambit positions (Greco 1620 game, the Hilton Wexler puzzle) are cheap hooks; none in Scholarmate [grep "Beth": 2 files, neither a lesson]. Mate-in-3 puzzle from the series is a clean checkable item (mate in 3 by Kd7; engine mate score at depth 18).
4. Pawn-structure breadth: Hellsten's 30 lessons vs Scholarmate's 9 structure skills. Missing by title: the closed Sicilian structure, the queenside majority structure, the Queen's Indian structure, and (from lesson lists not played) other named structures; also the "tactic that only exists because of the structure" item pairing seen in L16 and L30.
5. Weak color complexes: `grep` for "color complex" and "colour complex" over `content/` and `docs/` finds nothing outside the research notes; chess.com has a 22-lesson course on it (David Pruess, sampled earlier). Gap.
6. Event recaps (Candidates, Speed Chess Championship, Nations Cup): current-events content; none in Scholarmate; not recommended for v1 (dated) but a "famous tournament games" mini-strand per year could reuse the games.
7. Feedback design features seen here that Scholarmate should copy: amber for tied or near-tied moves with the author's idea named (Steinitz h6, Rapport h5, Beth b4, Firouzja Bg2); a mate-aware verdict ("winning, but there is no mate in three"); a hint that explains the position's quirk (Nepomniachtchi "odd-looking rook on g1"); stating the evaluation in words that match the engine (Gambit Refuted "White is doing well"); and a graded pair for the same sacrifice with a better piece (Closed Sicilian Ngf5+ against Nef5+). Defects to avoid: red for a 0.1-0.2 inaccuracy (Rapport L5, Speed Chess L20), "equally strong" at a 0.7 gap (2000s L1), "improve on the game" when the game move is the engine move (1970s L1), a bare "better option" with no hint (2010s L1), and wording that says "incorrect" for an engine-top move.
