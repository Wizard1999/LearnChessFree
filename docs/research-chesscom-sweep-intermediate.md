## Reading the Board, L6 "When to Trade Pieces" (reading-the-board/when-to-trade-pieces; 2018 Chess.com Team course, badge I; 7 challenges; challenges 1 to 3 played) [V]
Intro bubble: trading is helpful when you are ahead, but what if a trade does not win or lose material? (plus an Explainer Video, not watched). Layout is the old 2018 shell (Start (692,690), Next/Retry in one slot at (750,757), lightbulb hint at (732,757)).
- C1 `1r1r3k/p4pp1/1p2p2p/2p5/2P5/1P2P1P1/P2R1PKP/2R5 w`, "Both sides are fighting for the only open file. Is trading rooks White's best move?": accepted Rd1 (the rook behind the rook, the trade is Black's to make; engine +0.9, the other three top moves score about 0). The tempting Rxd8+ (a plain trade, engine about 0.0) was marked incorrect with a real reason: "This trade made White's position worse and the opponent's position better, as Black now controls the only open file". This is the best wrong-move text seen so far in the Team courses: it names the positional cost of the trade, not just "incorrect".
- C2 `1rbq1rk1/1pb2pp1/7p/p1nN4/P3p3/6P1/1PP1QPBP/R1B2RK1 w`, "Black is attacking White's knight on d5. Can you find a trade that will lead to winning material?": Nxc7 Qxc7 then the second move Bf4 (skewer on the queen with Rb8 behind). Engine +4.98 for Nxc7; Qc4 (engine +0.7, a decent move) was "incorrect: a much stronger capture to find". The explanation after Bf4 says White wins an entire rook; that overstates it, the realistic gain is the exchange (a rook for a bishop) [I, line not played out].
- C3 `r2r2k1/1bp2ppp/p3p3/1p1n4/3P4/2N3P1/PP3PBP/2BR2K1 b` (Black up a pawn and an exchange), "make our task easier by trading off White's active pieces": Nxc3 (bxc3), Bxg2 (Kxg2), then ...c5 attacking the d4 pawn that is pinned to the rook. Engine: Nxc3 +5.5 best. The winning alternative b4 (+4.3) was rejected with "this allows White to keep more active pieces ... we want to trade as many active white pieces as we can": grading follows the lesson concept (simplify when ahead), not just the engine, and says so. A three-move challenge; the hint button gives a one-word "Hint" bubble then on the second press highlights the piece and draws an arrow (so fewer steps in the ladder than Mastering the Endgame).
Findings [I]: (1) the concept lessons of the 2018 shell have the strongest explanations when the wrong move is a plausible concept error (trading when it helps the opponent); that is a model for Scholarmate's contrast lab, where a wrong choice is shown with the cost of that choice. (2) The grader rejects an engine-winning move because it contradicts the lesson's concept; acceptable when the prompt states the concept, but an uncommented rejection would be a defect. (3) Position quality: C1 to C3 are sound at depth 14. Challenges 4 to 7 not played; time about 12 minutes for three challenges with engine checks.
- C4 `r2qk2r/ppp2ppp/2np1n2/2b1p3/2B1P1b1/P1NP1N2/1PP2PPP/R1BQ1RK1 b` (Italian-type middlegame, Black pins the f3 knight), "Should Black capture the pinned knight, or can you add more pressure to this piece?" The "should you trade now?" question is asked twice in one challenge with different answers, which is a good contrast for a learner. Challenges 5 to 7 not played.
