## Cross-course patterns (Intermediate level, updated 9 Nov 2026, segment 6)
Feedback tiers: right / "is an alternative" (playable, a better move exists, the text often names the piece class or concept: "a pawn", "a capture", "control f5 with a pawn") / "is incorrect" (sometimes with the consequence, sometimes bare). Grading: the author's concept beats the engine (Hikaru, Pawn Play, Play Like Levy, Naroditsky), and the learner is never told when the engine disagrees. Hints: two to three presses (label, piece, arrow). Text volume: Silman and Trent open with 600 to 900 characters, Kane and Williams with one line, the Team 2018 series from nothing to 750 characters. Handoffs: the correct move triggers a follow-up question inside the same challenge. Best formats to borrow: "answer the why with the refuting move" (Weak Color Complexes), "what did White miss?" post-mortems (Illingworth), correct-answer text that lists the other accepted moves (Drawing Zone), the counterfactual "what if the sacrifice had been accepted?" (Hikaru), the three named defensive resources (Defending Difficult Positions). Defects: prompts that overstate ("win material" at +0.6), numbered lesson titles without topics, bare "incorrect".

## Reading the Board, L6 "When to Trade Pieces" (reading-the-board/when-to-trade-pieces; 2018 Chess.com Team course, badge I; 7 challenges; challenges 1 to 3 played) [V]
Intro bubble: trading is helpful when you are ahead, but what if a trade does not win or lose material? (plus an Explainer Video, not watched). Layout is the old 2018 shell (Start (692,690), Next/Retry in one slot at (750,757), lightbulb hint at (732,757)).
- C1 `1r1r3k/p4pp1/1p2p2p/2p5/2P5/1P2P1P1/P2R1PKP/2R5 w`, "Both sides are fighting for the only open file. Is trading rooks White's best move?": accepted Rd1 (the rook behind the rook, the trade is Black's to make; engine +0.9, the other three top moves score about 0). The tempting Rxd8+ (a plain trade, engine about 0.0) was marked incorrect with a real reason: "This trade made White's position worse and the opponent's position better, as Black now controls the only open file". This is the best wrong-move text seen so far in the Team courses: it names the positional cost of the trade, not just "incorrect".
- C2 `1rbq1rk1/1pb2pp1/7p/p1nN4/P3p3/6P1/1PP1QPBP/R1B2RK1 w`, "Black is attacking White's knight on d5. Can you find a trade that will lead to winning material?": Nxc7 Qxc7 then the second move Bf4 (skewer on the queen with Rb8 behind). Engine +4.98 for Nxc7; Qc4 (engine +0.7, a decent move) was "incorrect: a much stronger capture to find". The explanation after Bf4 says White wins an entire rook; that overstates it, the realistic gain is the exchange (a rook for a bishop) [I, line not played out].
- C3 `r2r2k1/1bp2ppp/p3p3/1p1n4/3P4/2N3P1/PP3PBP/2BR2K1 b` (Black up a pawn and an exchange), "make our task easier by trading off White's active pieces": Nxc3 (bxc3), Bxg2 (Kxg2), then ...c5 attacking the d4 pawn that is pinned to the rook. Engine: Nxc3 +5.5 best. The winning alternative b4 (+4.3) was rejected with "this allows White to keep more active pieces ... we want to trade as many active white pieces as we can": grading follows the lesson concept (simplify when ahead), not just the engine, and says so. A three-move challenge; the hint button gives a one-word "Hint" bubble then on the second press highlights the piece and draws an arrow (so fewer steps in the ladder than Mastering the Endgame).
Findings [I]: (1) the concept lessons of the 2018 shell have the strongest explanations when the wrong move is a plausible concept error (trading when it helps the opponent); that is a model for Scholarmate's contrast lab, where a wrong choice is shown with the cost of that choice. (2) The grader rejects an engine-winning move because it contradicts the lesson's concept; acceptable when the prompt states the concept, but an uncommented rejection would be a defect. (3) Position quality: C1 to C3 are sound at depth 14. Challenges 4 to 7 not played; time about 12 minutes for three challenges with engine checks.
- C4 `r2qk2r/ppp2ppp/2np1n2/2b1p3/2B1P1b1/P1NP1N2/1PP2PPP/R1BQ1RK1 b` (Italian-type middlegame, Black pins the f3 knight), "Should Black capture the pinned knight, or can you add more pressure to this piece?" ### Learning From Our Mistakes (GM Max Illingworth, 5 lessons, 25 challenges, 46 min, released 2019, badges I A M, topic Strategy) [V]

This is the closest competitor content to Scholarmate's "learn how to learn" goal; each lesson is a real game of the coach's own with five challenges, so the process lessons are carried by positions, not by worksheets [I from L1, L4 first screens only].
- L1 C1 `5r1k/4rppB/pN1p1b2/P2P4/R2q4/6P1/1PQ2P1P/6K1 b` (queen attacked): a two-move challenge. Qxa4 (the tempting capture, loses the queen to Nxa4) got only "Qxa4 is incorrect", no reason [V]. Re1+ was correct (engine +0.29, top), then after the scripted Kg2 the follow-up Qd1 (engine top again) got a good full explanation: the queen trade stops Bd3 and Black is slightly better, i.e. an honest, modest verdict.
- L1 C2 is the same game moved on: "What opportunity to win material did White miss here?" (white to move, FEN `5r1k/5ppB/pN1p1b2/P2Pq3/R7/6P1/1PQ2PKP/4r3 w`): the question is framed as a post-mortem of the coach's own game. Best Nd7 (engine +2.8). Bd3 (engine +1.6, a good move) got the three-way verdict "is an alternative": "a good position by attacking a6, but there is an even better move, try to find it" [V]. So the alternative tier exists in the new shell too.
- L4 "Losing Control Of Our Emotions" first challenge: "White just blundered. How can Black win material?" on a real game position, so the emotion lesson trains the habit (look for the opponent's blunder, do not flinch) rather than only talking. Not played further.
- Format: one real game per lesson, scripted opponent replies, challenge prompt written from the coach's viewpoint ("handle the threat to his queen"). No text walls here (1 to 2 lines per bubble). Handoff: Next to next challenge; end-of-lesson screens not reached.
- Idea for Scholarmate [I]: the post-mortem prompt "what did White miss here?" is a cheap, high-value format for the Games review page (ask the learner for the missed idea, then reveal). Defect: the bare "incorrect" on Qxa4 gives no consequence (the Nxa4 refutation is not shown).

### How to Be Lucky In Chess (GM Daniel Naroditsky, 5 lessons, 25 challenges, 69 min, badges I A M, Strategy) [V]
- Topic is time-pressure practicalities: flagging basics, time scrambles, swindles, practical decisions, bullet/blitz ideas. Unique among the courses seen: positions are chosen so that the clock, not the engine, decides the answer.
- L1 C1 `8/8/4b3/8/6k1/5p2/5Kp1/6B1 b` (Black bishop e6, king g4, pawns f3 g2 vs White Kf2 Bg1; prompt says both sides have seconds left and White premoves Bh2-g1-h2): Engine says everything is about equal (+0.04 best Bd7, +0.01 Bb3, 0.00 Bf7/Bc4). The intended answer is Kh3 (the hint arrow shows it), which makes White's premoved Bh2 illegal/losing so the clock-pressed opponent walks into g1=Q or loses the bishop [I]. Bd7, the engine's top move, was marked "incorrect: there is a more practical option": the grader encodes practical chances, not evaluation, and says so (the word "practical" is the signal). Hint ladder: bubble "Hint" then an arrow on the king: two steps.
- Finding for Scholarmate [I]: "practical vs objective" labelling is a gap worth an item type (the validator would need a claim like "the engine's best move is not the practical choice because the opponent's premove fails to X"). Not machine-checkable as an evaluation claim, so it would need a defined opponent model.
- Position quality: the C1 position is a legal endgame; the engine verdict contradicts the author unless the clock premise is accepted, which is a defect for any learner who checks it with an engine (no note in the lesson explaining this, only "more practical").

## Findings, segment 5 (five most useful)
1. Grading follows the lesson concept, not the engine, in the Silman, Naroditsky and Team courses: engine-sound moves (e4 +0.93, Bd7 +0.04, Bd3 +1.6, h6 +0.3) are marked incorrect or alternative. Reasons range from a full paragraph (Silman) to "there is a better move" (speed drills). The "alternative" tier appears in the new shell too.
2. Best wrong-move text found: Advanced Piece Values (names the material count) and Reading the Board L6 (names the positional cost). Worst: Illingworth L1 and Puzzle Rush (bare "incorrect").
3. Format worth adopting: "answer the why with the refuting move" (Weak Color Complexes: play the mate that shows why Qg5 fails) and "what did White miss here?" post-mortem prompts (Illingworth) for Games review.
4. Time-pressure/practical-choice items (Naroditsky: premove trick; Trent: bluff only when the downside is bounded) have no Scholarmate item type yet; the checkable claim is "the best opponent reply is only-move N times" via multipv, plus a stated downside ("draw with best play").
5. Text volume: Silman and Trent show 600 to 900-character coach paragraphs before the first move; Team and Puzzle Rush lessons show one line. Scholarmate's short-text approach is consistent with the Team style.
