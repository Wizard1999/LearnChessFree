# My own look at the icons (11 Nov 2026, D89c)

I opened all 155 pictures on four labelled contact sheets and read them against the skill map. Labels: [V] seen in the images, [H] my judgement.

## What the pictures are [V]
- **Concept objects that say the idea at once:** Pin (nail through teal cloth), Fork (pitchfork), Skewer (kebab on a spike), Defense (red shield), Calculation (abacus), Evaluation (scales), Time (hourglass), Out of Time (alarm clock), Tempo (metronome), Balance (stacked stones), Memory (card box), Repertoire (card chest), Trap (mousetrap), Check (rifle scope on the white king), Draw (handshake), Stalemate (tug of war), Piece Value (scales with a knight and pawns), Chess notation (note with e4 Nf3), Board coordinates (gridded map), Promotion (pawn beside a crown), Prophylaxis (hand bolting a window), Fortress (castle), Weakness (cracked chain), Space (figure in a big hall).
- **Twelve piece portraits** (white and black king, queen, rook, bishop, knight, pawn): plain and clear; the right icons for "How the X moves".
- **People-in-a-scene pictures** that need a story: Hidden danger (woman and snake), Checkmate (frightened king, spear), Sacrifice (bird shielding a chick, or a knight), Passed pawn (runners), Pawn chain (climbers), Triangulation (fawn between wolves), Help (rescue hands), Clearance (crowd). They are vivid but slower to read.
- **App-level pictures that are not chess skills** and are free to use for the school itself: Lesson (red book), Review (open book), Practice (pottery wheel), Mastery (graduation cap), Reward (medal), Progress (stairs), Recovery (shoot on a stump), Persistence (sprout through rock), Reflection (mirror, quill, pawn), Observation (magnifier), Compass (direction), Hint (question mark on a scroll), Focus, Escape, Lightbulb, Mating net.
- Several tiles carry no skill and no assignment (Lightbulb, Sneakers, Teacup, Plank bridge, Stepping stones, Folding fan, Magnet and key, Boot and stone, Drawn bow, Striking viper): unused material.

## Where I would use them, beyond the skill map [H]
1. **Skill and lesson lists, skill panel (done, every stage, at least 32 px).**
2. **Page and card marks that need no skill:** Practice page = Practice (pottery), Review/spaced review = Memory or Review, Lesson cards = Lesson, Games review = Reflection, Openings = Repertoire, Placement = Compass; milestone cards: first lesson = Lesson, level Guided = Progress, Mastered = Mastery, finished stage = Reward, whole core = Reward. The Hint button could carry Hint (question mark): a hint is asked for by the learner, so it does not give the answer away.
3. **After the answer only:** the skill tag in Practice, Contrast lab and Games review.
4. **Never:** lesson boards, the first guided game, before an answer, because a tactic icon tells you the technique.

## Mistakes found in the proposed map and what I did [V/H]
- **Rank** is a stack of books, so the seventh-rank lesson now shows no icon (override).
- **Mating without stalemating** showed Checkmate; it now shows Stalemate. **En passant, Desperado, Multi-motif combinations, What does this pawn move leave behind?** show none (see D89c).
- **Dedicated drafts exist and look good**, and are not used until Andrew approves them: **En Passant** (two fencers, a blade through the sash: the best of them), **Good and Bad Bishops** (one dove flying, one caged), **Exchange Sacrifice** (a rook on the scale against a knight and a key), **Perpetual Check** (a king between cobras), **Battery**, **Hanging Pawns**. If approved they replace the weaker stand-ins (Evaluation for good and bad pieces, the bird for the exchange sacrifice, Check for perpetual check, Capture for en passant).
- **Shared pictures:** Checkmate is on ten skills, Weakness on eight, Calculation and Evaluation on seven each, so these mark a family of ideas and cannot tell two skills apart; the app already draws them a little smaller.
- **Content that may be too strong for the youngest learners:** Checkmate (a terrified king with a spear at him), Hidden danger (snake and a woman). Shown at Stage 2 for Checkmate in one: worth Andrew's look.
