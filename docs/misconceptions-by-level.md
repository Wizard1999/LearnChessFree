# Common misconceptions by level

Written 26 October 2026 from general knowledge of coaching and learner patterns **[R: recalled, not checked against a source in this session]**. Reddit is blocked in my browser tool and I have not read forum threads for this, so treat the list as a first draft to compare against real learner data (the app's own wrong-answer logs will do that better once there are testers). Each line says what the learner believes, and the kind of exercise that exposes it. All 204 skill files already carry a `misconceptions` list; this note is the cross-cutting view, to check that the practice bank actually tests each one.

## Beginners (new to the rules, up to about 800)
- "A piece is safe if it is defended once." Exercise: count attackers and defenders where the cheaper attacker wins. (SAF.COUNT)
- "Check means the game is nearly over", so checks are played just because they are checks. Exercise: positions where the check loses the checking piece.
- "Castling is always allowed." Exercise: castling through, out of, or into check; after the king or rook moved.
- "A stalemate is a win for the stronger side / a loss for the stalemated one." Exercise: win or draw classification. (FND.STALEMATE)
- "The queen should come out early." Exercise: early queen sortie punished by developing moves with tempo.
- "Trading when ahead is always good; trading when behind is always bad." Exercise: trades that give back the advantage, and trades that help the defender.
- "My opponent's last move does nothing." Exercise: the opponent's quiet move that creates a threat. (LOOP.V1)

## Improving players (about 800 to 1400)
- "A pin means the pinned piece cannot move." Exercise: relative against absolute pins, and counter-captures.
- "A fork needs a knight." Exercise: pawn, bishop, queen and king forks.
- "I should always capture material that is offered." Exercise: poisoned pawn and removal-of-defender positions.
- "A knight on the rim is just a bit worse." Exercise: rim knights with no good squares.
- "Rooks belong on open files, so any open file will do." Exercise: an open file the opponent can contest, against one that leads to an entry square.
- "A passed pawn is always strong." Exercise: a passed pawn that is blockaded or weak, against a protected one.
- "An engine's top move is the only move." Exercise: find-the-move items with several sound moves (the app already accepts engine-equal alternatives).
- "More moves of calculation is better than a clear candidate list." Exercise: positions where the first candidate move was never considered. (CAL.CANDIDATES)

## Club players (about 1400 to 1900)
- "Bishops are better than knights." Exercise: closed positions where the knight outperforms the bishop.
- "Bad bishop means a bad position." Exercise: a bad bishop that is protecting the structure, and one that can be traded.
- "An isolated pawn is always a weakness." Exercise: IQP positions with enough activity. (POS.STRUCTURE.IQP)
- "I should attack on the side where I have more pieces regardless of the king." Exercise: counterattack in the centre when the flank attack is premature.
- "A sacrifice is justified if I cannot see the refutation." Exercise: sacrifices whose refutation is a quiet move.
- "Draws are failures; I should play on." Exercise: perpetual, fortress and stalemate resources. (PRA.RESIST)
- "Opposition always decides the pawn ending." Exercise: positions where the opposition is the wrong idea (key squares, triangulation, outside passer).
- "Rook behind the passed pawn is always right." Exercise: the exceptions, using tablebase-checked positions.

## Advanced learners (above about 1900)
- "Prophylaxis is passive." Exercise: moves that stop the opponent's best plan and also improve the position.
- "Evaluation equals material plus activity." Exercise: positions where structure or time decides.
- "I know this opening line, so I can skip understanding it." Exercise: deviation by the opponent on move N, where the plan matters more than the move.
- "Long calculation is rigorous even when I am tired." Exercise: error rate against line length, in the learner's own log. (CAL.ENDURANCE)
