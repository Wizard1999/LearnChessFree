# Synthesis sheet: F. M. Edge, *The Exploits and Triumphs, in Europe, of Paul Morphy* (1859)

Sheet author: reading agent (Sonnet 5.5), 2026-10-29. Source: `library/files/pg34180.txt` (Project Gutenberg #34180).

**Label key.** [R] = recalled from the text I read. [I] = my inference or outside knowledge, not from this book. [V] = checked here with a tool. No sentence in the book is quoted beyond a few words.

## 1. Scope and edition

- **Work.** Frederick Milnes Edge, who signs as Morphy's secretary. New York: D. Appleton, 1859, with an 1859 US copyright entry. Gutenberg release 1 Nov 2010, volunteer-produced. [R] A work from 1859 is public domain in the US and, with an author who died long ago, almost certainly elsewhere [I]. Rights are not a concern for facts; wording must still be our own.
- **Genre.** A travel-and-gossip chronicle of Morphy's 1858 European tour, plus a history of London chess clubs, the New York congress of 1857, and a long dossier on the failed Staunton match. [R]
- **Critical fact for our use: the book prints no game scores and no diagrams.** I searched the whole file for algebraic and descriptive move notation and found only a few spoken phrases, such as the opening move of a blindfold display. [R, V by search] Every "game" below is therefore a game *mentioned*, not a game *given*. It cannot feed the famous-games course by itself.
- **Partiality.** The author is Morphy's friend and the opponent of Staunton; the Staunton chapter is advocacy. [R] Treat all strength judgments as the author's view.

## 2. What the book teaches, in order

1. Ch. I-II: childhood in New Orleans; the First American Chess Congress, New York, Oct 1857, where he won the knock-out final against Paulsen (five games, one draw, one loss). [R]
2. Ch. III-IV: the English doubt his strength; a history of the London Divan, Westminster, St. George's, London Chess Club, Philidorean Rooms, and earlier players (Philidor, Labourdonnais, McDonnell, Lewis). [R]
3. Ch. V: England 1858: matches, off-hand games, the Birmingham meeting and eight-board blindfold display. [R]
4. Ch. VI: the Staunton correspondence and why no match took place. [R]
5. Ch. VII-XI: Paris, the Cafe de la Regence, the Harrwitz match, the Paris eight-board blindfold display. [R]
6. Ch. XII-XIII: society games and the Paris amateurs. [R]
7. Ch. XIV-XVI: persuading Morphy to stay; the Anderssen match; the Mongredieu match. [R]
8. Ch. XVII: a table of results; the closing tribute. [R]

It is not a chess-instruction book. Its few "principles" are remarks on temperament, preparation and fatigue (§3).

## 3. Ideas, with conditions and exceptions

All are the author's reported views or anecdotes. None has a move-level check, because no moves are given.

- **I1. Play the best move, not the brilliant one.** Anderssen is reported to say that Morphy always finds the very best move, so an only approximately correct reply loses. [R] The Paris players called his style sound and close; English players called it brilliant; the author says the difference came from the Parisians insisting on closed openings. [R] Condition: this is style testimony, not analysis. [I] fits CAL.CANDIDATES / CAL.DEFENSE as motivation only.
- **I2. Go straight to the finish.** In a game against de Riviere, Morphy was worse, then ignored a showy move and worked clear to a win; the author says he allows no liberties with mate. [R] Exception visible in the same book: in the Harrwitz match he lost a rook through a careless move while winning, and had to take a perpetual check (the only draw of that match). [R] Also in 1857 he spoiled a long combination against Paulsen by playing fast and drew from a lost position. [R] Lesson: speed once "certain" is where errors enter (fits PRA.SECURE).
- **I3. Resistance when worse.** Against Harrwitz's Allgaier Gambit he lost three pawns early and still fought for close to a hundred moves, though he lost. [R] Fits PRA.RESIST, with the caveat that the book does not show his defensive method.
- **I4. Fatigue and rest affect results.** The author argues Morphy lost the first two Harrwitz games after late nights, and that the rest he enforced preceded the later wins; Morphy himself would not admit it. [R] Also illness before the Anderssen match; he lost the first game after weeks in bed. [R] This is the author's causal story; it cannot be tested. [I] Fits PRA.LOAD and CAL.ENDURANCE as a prompt for a learner's own log, not as evidence.
- **I5. Blindfold ability is a display, not a measure.** Morphy called it a mere show that proves nothing, per the author. [R] The author contrasts Paulsen's long, slow blindfold games with Morphy's shorter, sharper ones. [R] Fits CAL.BLIND and CAL.VISUALIZE (a limit on how far to push it: opponents may blunder; Anderssen said Harrwitz's blindfold opponents often left pieces hanging). [R]
- **I6. Opening knowledge matters for results (rival view).** New York players said Morphy beat them through better opening knowledge; the author rejects this. [R] Morphy's own claim that the Evans Gambit is a loss for White against careful defence (after his loss to Anderssen) is reported at second hand. [R]
- **I7. Odds as a rating scale.** Strength is expressed as odds (pawn and move, pawn and two moves, knight, rook). [R] The final table claims he could give pawn and move to anyone. [R] Useful as history for GAME.HISTORY; unrelated to our evidence-graded ratings (project rule 6).
- **I8. Courtesy and conduct.** Contrast between Morphy's behavior and Harrwitz's (pulse-taking taunt; moving the match; resigning for "ill health"; claiming afterward it was annulled). [R] Partisan source; fits PRA.COMPETE / PRA.RECOVER only as discussion material.

## 6. Gaps the graph has and the book fills

- The graph has no node for **the history of rating by odds** or for how strength was judged before ratings; the book describes it at length. GAME.HISTORY is the nearest. [I]
- Nothing in the graph covers **tournament versus match temperament**, or whether results at the time represented true strength; Morphy skipped the Birmingham tournament on the view that tournament results do not measure strength. [R] This is a motive, not a fact to teach.
- The book fills **no chess-content gap**. All real content (attack, initiative, development) must come from game scores in other sources.

## 7. Outdated or doubtful claims, and what I checked

- **"The Evans Gambit is lost for White against correct defence."** Morphy's view as reported. [R] Engine check [V]: with the stockfish 19 lite single-thread build, depth 22, after 1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.Bc4 Bc5 4.b4 Bxb4 5.c3 Ba5 6.d4 exd4 7.O-O (Black to move) the score is about +0.08 for the side to move, which is roughly level, not lost for White; after 7...d3 it is about +0.42 for White. One line, one depth, one engine; not a verdict on the opening. The claim as stated is too strong. [V]
- **Harrwitz's "1.d4 and Philidor Defence" as match openings.** The author's description of habits, unchecked. For comparison, the same engine rates 1.d4 d5 about +0.28 for White at depth 20 [V]; no inference about the match.
- **"Morphy beats everyone through brilliancy"** versus "through sound play": opposing impressions reported by the author; unverifiable without scores.
- **Match totals** disagree slightly in kind: the text says Anderssen "lost two, drew two" while the table lists 7-2-2, consistent. The text says Harrwitz resigned at 5-2-1. [R] I found no internal contradiction in the scores I read.
- **The claim that Staunton would have lost, and that the delay was evasion,** is the author's argument. Not assessable here.
- **Modern evaluation of Morphy's strength** is outside the book; do not cite the author's ranking of him above all players as fact.
